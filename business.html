    Ask it to interview you

    Use it when: you don’t know where to start.

    You are my business adviser and you know AI for small companies. This is my problem: [tell it the messy way].

Before proposing anything, ask me questions, at most 5, one at a time. Include what I already tried and how it went, what I can't change and what I want in three months.

Then give me 3 places where AI would help, from easiest to hardest, with no technical words, and tell me which decisions are mine.
    Check
    That it doesn’t recommend something you said had already failed.
    In my test
    In 48 conversations with this version, it used what had already been tried in 34. Without the three required questions, in 5 of 48.

    Find the weakest part of my plan

    Use it when: you are about to put money or time into something.

    I'm about to make this decision: [decision]. What I know: [facts]. What I assume: [assumptions].

Don't tell me if it's a good idea. List what would have to be true for it to work. Order it from most doubtful to most certain and explain why.

For the two most doubtful points, tell me how to check them in one week with little money, and what result should make me stop. If you are missing a fact, ask for it. Don't assume it.
    Check
    That the result that makes you stop is something you can count.
    In my test
    I tried it 10 times with an invented bakery. It always put my guesses at the top of the doubtful list. I did not check its advice with a real business.

    From a messy message to an order

    Use it when: a client sends a voice note or a long message.

    I'm going to paste a client's message. Make a table with: product, quantity, date, address and payment method.

Use only what it says. If something is not there, write "not said". If something can be understood in two ways, write both.

Then write one short message for the client asking for what is missing.

Message: [paste it without names or phone numbers]
    Check
    The quantities, one by one, against the original message.
    In my test
    10 tries with “3 or 4 dozen, the usual ones”. In the 10 it kept “3 or 4” and asked, instead of choosing.

    Answer “it’s too expensive” without lowering the price

    Use it when: a client asks for a discount and your rule doesn’t allow it.

    A client says my price is high. I sell: [product and price]. What it includes: [list]. My discount rule: [write it, or "none"]. What they said: [message].

Write two answers of under 70 words. One asks a question to understand what they are comparing with. The other offers a smaller version of what I sell, using only what my list includes.

Don't offer discounts, longer terms or gifts. Don't speak badly of others. Don't write prices I didn't give you.
    Check
    That no new price shows up.
    In my test
    10 tries. No new price and no discount appeared. Sometimes it leaves a [price] for you to fill in.

    A post that sounds like you

    Use it when: you have something to tell and it always comes out like a brochure.

    These are three texts I wrote: [paste them]. Read them and tell me in five lines how I write: length of sentences, words I repeat, what I never do.

Now write a post about: [topic]. Facts you can use: [facts]. Don't use any others. Don't say what my clients ask for, wonder or feel if it is not in the facts. Under 120 words.

At the end, mark the sentence that sounds least like me and propose another one.
    Check
    That it doesn’t tell something that never happened to you.
    In my test
    My first version invented what clients wanted in 4 of 10 texts. I added the sentence about clients and it went to 0 of 20.

    Answer a claim

    Use it when: someone is upset, and so are you.

    A client wrote this: [message]. What happened according to us: [facts]. What I can offer: [list, or "nothing yet"].

Write an answer of under 90 words. First acknowledge what happened to them, with no excuses. Then say what comes next and when. Offer only what is on my list.

Apart from that, write what I have to decide before sending it.
    Check
    Dates and promises. They will hold you to those.
    In my test
    10 tries. It offered only the two dates on the list. It also said “right away”, and that is a promise too.

    What repeats in the comments

    Use it when: you have twenty reviews or more and don’t know what to fix first.

    I'm going to paste client comments, one per line. Group them by topic. For each topic tell me how many there are, the line numbers and two sentences copied exactly.

A comment can be in two topics. If it fits in none, put it in "others".

Topics with fewer than three comments go apart, as "to look at". Don't give me recommendations yet.

Comments: [paste them without names]
    Check
    Count one topic yourself. If that number is wrong, don’t use the others.
    In my test
    10 tries with 20 comments. It found the 4 about payment every time. The ones about waiting were 5 or 6, depending on the model.

    Collect without hurting the relationship

    Use it when: an invoice is overdue and you feel bad writing.

    I have to collect an invoice of [amount], [days] days overdue. My relationship with the client: [tell it]. How it can be paid: [ways].

Write three messages of under 60 words: one that assumes they forgot, one that asks if there was a problem and one that asks for a specific date.

Don't threaten, don't mention credit reports or interest, and don't offer discounts or more time.
    Check
    The amount and the days. Pick only one message.
    In my test
    10 tries. No threats, no interest, no extra time.

    Compare supplier quotes

    Use it when: you have three quotes and each one comes in a different way.

    I'm going to paste quotes from several suppliers. Make a table with: what is included, what is not, price as it appears, whether it has taxes, delivery time, payment terms and validity.

If something is missing, write "not said". Don't calculate totals or convert units. Tell me which calculations I should do in a spreadsheet to be able to compare them.

Then write the missing questions for each supplier.

Quotes: [paste them]
    Check
    That prices are the same as in the document.
    In my test
    10 tries with three quotes. The prices were copied exactly in the 10. One answer wrote an approximate calculation I had not asked for.

    Get a procedure out of your head

    Use it when: only one person knows how to do something.

    I want to write down how this is done: [task]. Interview me, one question at a time and eight at most. Ask how it starts, what is needed, the steps, what I do when it goes wrong, what must never be done and how I know it came out right.

At the end write the procedure with numbered steps that a new person can follow. Use only what I said. Where something is missing, write [MISSING] and what it is.
    Check
    Give it to someone who has never done it and see where they stop.
    In my test
    I have not measured this one. It is a conversation and it depends on what you answer.

    How to install it

    1. Download the zip and open it. Read the SKILL.md and change the prices, rules or policy for yours.
    2. Zip the folder again, with the same name.
    3. In Claude, turn on code execution in Settings, Capabilities.
    4. Go to Customize, Skills, press +, then Create skill and Upload a skill.
    5. Try it with three old cases before you trust it.

    In another assistant, paste the text of SKILL.md as instructions. The programs need an assistant that can run Python.

    I ran each one on three cases (a normal one, one with an exception and a strange one) with two models. The figures came from the program when it could run. In one answer the figures were right and the sentence that explained them was not, so read both.

    What I have tested

    When it could not ask, 298 of 300 quotes followed a rule that nobody wrote.

    A vague discount rule and 1,200 quotes: what three models decided

    It asked 240 questions. None was about what had been tried before.

    A prompt that asks before it advises: 144 conversations with six invented businesses

    Of 50 names, the tool covered 10.

    Before you paste your client list into a chat

    The smallest model got 21 of 40 totals. The program got all 40.

    Let the model read and a program add: 40 documents with three models

    With the handbook, the older model went from 21 made up answers to 7, out of 45.

    A university assistant with and without the handbook: 270 answers checked one by one
